## Technique: overlap + pin + reveal through the hole

1. **Overlap, no spacer.** `.js #hero.board-hero{margin-bottom:-100svh}`, so `#main` sits on top of the last screen of
   the 260vh runway (`position:relative; z-index:1`). `#main`'s natural top reaches the viewport top at exactly
   `scrollY = main.offsetTop`, which is the same moment the sticky stage lets go. Document height and anchors stay normal.
2. **Pin while flying.** From fly-in progress `p = 0.30` until `scrollY = main.offsetTop`, `hand = smoothstep(...)` and
   `#main.handoff` gets `transform: translateY(-natTop) scale(s)`, with `s = 0.42 → 1` and `transform-origin` = hole centre
   (`view.cx, view.cy`). The page stays at the viewport top and moves toward the viewer out of the hole.
3. **Clip to the hole.** `clip-path: circle(RH·view.R / s at view.cx view.cy)` is in #main's pre-scale coordinates, so on
   screen it is exactly the D's hole. It becomes `none` once the hole covers the viewport.
4. **Dissolve the D.** `#main` opacity goes 0 → 1 over the first 22% of `hand`, and stage opacity goes 1 → 0 over
   `hand` .35 → 1, onto the shared `#0D0D0D` ground (`#main.handoff` background).
5. **Invisible release.** At `scrollY = main.offsetTop` the translate is 0 and the scale is 1, so the styles are cleared
   and normal scrolling continues. Measured: the frame at the release point vs. 1px later has a mean |Δ| of 0.08/255 after a 1px shift (desktop
   1306×793 and mobile 390×844).

**Why `p = 0.30` and not ~0.72:** with zoom `1+13·e`, the hole (radius `RH·R`) is already bigger than the viewport by
p ≈ 0.5. A reveal that starts at 0.72 would be one frame from nothing to everything, after ~0.2 of black
runway. Starting at 0.30, the hole is still framed by the D's rim (~400px radius on desktop) when the page first shows inside it.

## Guards

- Reduced motion: hero 100vh, `margin-bottom:0`, handoff never runs.
- No JS: no `.js` class, no overlap. The board hero scrolls away and `#main` follows normally.
- Keyboard: `focusin` inside `#main` mid-flight jumps to `main.offsetTop` (end of the flight), so focus never lands on scaled or clipped content.
- site.js's `[data-alt]` page readout would grab the hero ALT, so the hero uses `[data-hero-alt]`.

Verified locally (python -m http.server + headless Chrome, SwiftShader WebGL): no console errors at 1306×793 or 390×844.
Tested reduced motion, the no-WebGL fallback (`#dfbPath` drawn), and scrolling back to the top (styles cleared).
